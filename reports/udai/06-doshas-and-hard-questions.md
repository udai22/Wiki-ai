# 06 · Doshas and the hard questions

*Udai · 22 Jun 1999, 21:35 IST, Mumbai · Capricorn lagna, Virgo Moon in Chitra 2. Written 18 Oct 2026, at age 27.*

This chapter has two parts.

- **Part A** checks every dosha that people, matchmakers and websites commonly raise. For each one it gives the variant rules, the cancellation rules, and where the masters disagree.
- **Part B** prepares answers to the questions a person most often asks.

The tags are the ones used in the [master map](00-master-map.md):
- **[Robust]** holds from about 20:51 to 22:41 (−43.5 to +66 min).
- **[Moderate]** means the dates slide but the conclusion holds.
- **[Time-sensitive]** depends on the exact minute.

Two ground rules. A dosha is a *named pattern* in a text, not a sentence passed on a person. And nothing here predicts death, longevity or serious illness. The classical texts that discuss those topics also warn against announcing them, and the evidence base for doing so is nil.

# Part A · Doshas

## A1. Manglik (Kuja dosha)

**The rule.** Mars in the 1st, 4th, 7th, 8th or 12th house counts as dosha. South Indian practice, following the commonly quoted Kerala verse, adds the **2nd**. The count is made from three points, with weight falling in this order: **lagna > Moon > Venus**. Many North Indian matchmakers use the lagna alone, or the lagna and the Moon.

**The counts for this chart.** Mars is at 2°41′ Libra.

| Reference | Mars is… | North list (1, 4, 7, 8, 12) | South list (adds the 2nd) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lagna (Capricorn) | **10th** | No | No |
| Moon (Virgo) | **2nd** | No | **Yes** |
| Venus (Cancer) | **4th** | **Yes** | Yes |

Two cross-checks agree. With equal houses from the ascendant degree, Mars is still in the 10th. On Placidus/KP cusps Mars falls in the **9th**, because the sidereal 10th cusp is at about 23° Libra. Neither house is a Manglik house. So the primary count is clean under every house system.

**The cancellation rules (bhanga) and how each applies:**

| Cancellation (source type) | Applies? |
|---|---|
| Jupiter conjoins or aspects Mars (widely cited in matching manuals) | **Yes.** Jupiter in Aries casts its 7th aspect onto Libra. This is the strongest cancellation in the chart. |
| The sign-specific list: Mars in the 2nd in Gemini or Virgo, the 4th in Aries or Scorpio, the 7th in Cancer or Capricorn, the 8th in Sagittarius or Pisces, the 12th in Taurus or Libra | Partly. Libra is excused only *in the 12th*. Mars is 2nd from the Moon and 4th from Venus, which are not the excused combinations. |
| Mars in a **Venus sign (Taurus/Libra)** anywhere: a broader rule in some compilations | **Yes** under that broader list. Other lists confine it to the 12th. |
| Mars in its own or exaltation sign, or in Cancer/Leo | No |
| A strong 7th lord in a kendra or trikona; benefics in the 7th | **Yes.** The Moon (7th lord) is bright and vargottama in the 9th, a trikona. Venus and Mercury occupy the 7th. |
| Saturn aspecting Mars (in some lists) | **Yes.** Saturn's 7th aspect falls on Libra. |
| The partner has an equivalent Mars (*samya*) | Depends on the match |
| **The age-28 rule:** Mars "matures" at 28, so the dosha "fades" | Udai turns 28 on **22 Jun 2027**. This comes from later tradition on planetary maturation ages. It is not in BPHS. |

**Where the masters disagree.**
- **B.V. Raman** treated Kuja dosha as a real matching factor. In his matching writings he also stressed how many exceptions exist, and he argued it was over-applied. He wanted it judged with the whole 7th house, not a single placement.
- **K.N. Rao** went further. He repeatedly warned against mechanical dosha lists, and said that the 7th house, its lord, Venus and the navamsa decide married life. A sign count does not.
- Both men would also notice that this Mars is *dignified*. It has dig bala in the 10th, it is vargottama, and it sits in its own nakshatra (Chitra). Mechanical lists ignore all of that. Some readers say a strong Mars makes any dosha more intense. Others say a dignified Mars harms less. The texts don't settle it.

**Verdict [Robust]: not Manglik by the primary (lagna) count.** At most this is a *partial* ("anshik") Manglik by the secondary Moon (South list) and Venus counts, and those are cancelled by Jupiter's aspect on Mars. The positions involved are lagna sign, Moon sign and Venus sign, so the verdict doesn't depend on the birth minute. In a traditional matching, an honest astrologer would write "Kuja dosha: not present / cancelled". A website using the Venus count alone may print "Manglik". That is the source of most confusion.

## A2. Kaal Sarp

**The test.** Are all seven planets hemmed between Rahu (20°05′ Cancer) and Ketu (20°05′ Capricorn)?

| Arc | Planets |
|---|---|
| Rahu → Ketu (Cancer 20° → Leo → … → Capricorn 20°) | **Venus** (1°44′ past Rahu), **Moon**, **Mars** |
| Ketu → Rahu (Capricorn 20° → … → Cancer 20°) | Jupiter, Saturn, Sun, **Mercury** (1°28′ Cancer, just short of Rahu) |

**Verdict [Robust]: no Kaal Sarp.** The planets split 3–4 across the axis, so the pattern isn't even close.

**Why some people will still claim it:**
- **Name by axis alone.** The twelve "types" are named purely by which houses the nodes occupy. Rahu in the 7th with Ketu in the 1st is the "**Takshak**" type. Some sellers label any chart by its node axis without running the hemming test at all.
- **"Partial" (anshik) Kaal Sarp.** Some claim it when one planet escapes, or when a planet conjoins a node. Here three planets escape, so even the generous version fails.
- **Degree-based variants.** Some count a planet as "inside" if it sits behind a node by degree in the same sign. That would put Venus "in the serpent's mouth".

**Where the masters disagree.** Kaal Sarp does not appear in BPHS, Brihat Jataka, Saravali or Phaladeepika. **K.N. Rao** wrote a book-length rebuttal of the fear built around it (*Kalsarpa Yoga: Why Such Fear Psychosis?*). The BPHS *Sarpa* yoga is a different thing entirely: a Nabhasa yoga with malefics in three kendras. It is absent here too, because the Sun is not in a kendra.

The real nodal story in this chart is **Rahu 1°44′ from Venus**, and the [Parashari chapter](01-parashari.md) treats it properly as a question of modifying a yoga, not as a curse.

## A4. Gandanta, for every point

Gandanta is the water-to-fire junction: Pisces/Aries, Cancer/Leo and Scorpio/Sagittarius. The authors use different orbs:
- **Tight:** 48′ to 1° either side, used especially for the lagna.
- **Standard:** one pada, 3°20′, which gives Revati 4/Ashwini 1, Ashlesha 4/Magha 1 and Jyeshtha 4/Mula 1.
- **Widest:** some modern writers stretch the zone to the *whole* junction nakshatras, borrowing the muhurta "gandamoola" list of Ashwini, Ashlesha, Magha, Jyeshtha, Mula and Revati.

| Point | Distance to the nearest junction | Tight | 1 pada | Whole-nakshatra stretch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Jupiter** 5°12′ Aries (Ashwini 2) | **5°12′** past Pisces/Aries | No | **No** (pada 1 ends at 3°20′) | Yes |
| Venus 21°49′ Cancer (Ashlesha 2) | 8°11′ before Cancer/Leo | No | No (Ashlesha 4 starts at 26°40′) | Yes |
| Rahu 20°05′ Cancer (Ashlesha 2) | 9°55′ | No | No | Yes |
| Saturn 19°34′ Aries | 19°34′ | No | No | No (Bharani) |
| Mercury 1°28′ Cancer | 28°32′ (Gemini/Cancer is air/water, not gandanta) | No | No | No |
| Sun, Moon, Mars, Ketu, lagna | ≥ 41° | No | No | No |

**Verdict [Robust]: no planet and no lagna is in gandanta under the classical orbs.** Jupiter sits one pada clear of the standard zone. Only the stretched, whole-nakshatra reading pulls in Jupiter, Venus and Rahu, and that reading takes a rule meant for the *Moon's birth star* and applies it to planets, which no classical text does. The Moon's own nakshatra, Chitra, is not a gandamoola star, so no gandamoola shanti is called for.

Mercury (1°28′ Cancer) and the Moon (1°28′ from the end of Virgo) are both near sign edges. That is *rashi sandhi* in some systems, which is read as weakness of expression and not as dosha.

## A7. Nadi dosha and the matching profile

Nadi dosha is a *matching* concept. It does not exist in a single chart.

- **Udai's Moon nakshatra, Chitra, is Madhya (Pitta) nadi.** Nadi dosha would arise only with a partner whose Moon is also in a Madhya-nadi star: Bharani, Mrigashira, Pushya, Purva Phalguni, Chitra, Anuradha, Purva Ashadha, Dhanishta or Uttara Bhadrapada.
- **The standard exceptions:** same nakshatra in a different rashi (Chitra spans Virgo and Libra), same rashi in different nakshatras, same nakshatra in a different pada (in some lists), and friendly or identical Moon-sign lords.
- **Other matching labels that will come up:**
  - Chitra is **Rakshasa gana**. Some matchers over-weight a Rakshasa–Deva pairing.
  - The yoni is the tiger.
  - The Virgo Moon gives a human (manava) vashya.
- **The disagreement.** Ashtakoota allots its largest single score, 8 of 36, to nadi. Many practitioners, Rao among them, treat a whole-chart comparison (7th houses, Venus, D9, dashas running at marriage) as far more important than the koota total.

## A8. Remedial measures, as the traditions list them

This section reports what traditions prescribe. It is **not a recommendation**. No controlled evidence supports gemstones or ritual remedies having physical effects, and the commercial incentives are large.

**Gemstones (the lagna-based school, the most common modern practice).** Stones go to the lagna lord and the functional benefics.
- **For Capricorn:** blue sapphire (*neelam*) for Saturn, the lagna lord; diamond or white sapphire for Venus, the yogakaraka; emerald for Mercury, the 9th lord.
- **Avoid:** the functional malefics, meaning yellow sapphire (Jupiter, 3rd/12th lord), red coral (Mars) and pearl (Moon, 7th lord and maraka).

**The debate specific to this chart:**
- **Saturn's stone for a debilitated lagna lord.**
  - One camp says: always strengthen the lagna lord.
  - A second camp says: a gem strengthens a planet's *expression*, and a debilitated planet's expression is its weakness.
  - A third camp says: neecha-bhanga converts the planet, so strengthening is safe.
  - Folk practice adds a "trial wear" of neelam because of its reputation for fast effects, which is anecdotal.
- **Venus's stone with Rahu 1°44′ away.** Some argue that boosting Venus also feeds Rahu's appetite. Others see this as exactly the yoga-giving node that BPHS ch. 34 describes, which should be supported.
- **Jupiter, the running mahadasha lord.** Gem sellers often push yellow sapphire during Jupiter MD. Strict lagna-based practice says no, because Jupiter rules the 3rd and 12th.

**Mantra and devata (BPHS remedial chapters; SJC).**
- Navagraha japa for the afflicted or dasha-running planet.
- **Sanjay Rath's SJC tradition** puts mantra, *ishta devata* worship and charity ahead of gems, and is openly critical of gem prescriptions handed out routinely. From the karakamsa ([ch. 02](02-jaimini.md)), the 12th from the karakamsa points through the Sun to **Shiva or Surya-Narayana/Rama** as ishta devata. The 9th from the karakamsa points to a guru or Dattatreya form.
- Hanuman worship on Tuesdays is the folk remedy attached to Mars and Kuja dosha.

**Charity (dāna).**
- Saturn: black sesame, iron, oil, and service to the elderly or labourers, on Saturdays.
- Rahu: Durga worship, and help for outsiders and migrants.
- Ketu: help for spiritual institutions.

**Contested rituals.**
- **Kumbh vivah** ("marriage" to a pot or peepal tree before marrying a Manglik) is folk practice with no classical basis, and is unnecessary here in any case.
- **Kaal Sarp puja** (at Trimbakeshwar and elsewhere) addresses a dosha that classical texts don't contain and this chart doesn't have.

**The rationalist and Osho view.** Paracelsus's *sapiens dominabitur astris* and Osho's teaching both place the remedy in awareness and conduct. Saturn "remedied" by discipline, punctuality and service is the one prescription that every school would endorse and that costs nothing.

## B12. "Is astrology real?"

The honest answer comes in two parts.

**What the tests show:**
- **Carlson (1985, *Nature* 318).** Astrologers, in a double-blind design they had agreed to beforehand, matched charts to personality profiles at chance level. Ertel's later re-analysis argued for a marginal effect in one sub-test. That has not changed the overall reading.
- **Dean & Kelly (2003, *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 10).** About 2,000 "time twins" born minutes apart in London in 1958 showed no meaningful similarity across roughly 100 measured traits. That is a direct test of the premise this whole report uses for birth-time sensitivity.
- **Narlikar and colleagues (2009, *Current Science*).** Vedic astrologers could not tell the charts of gifted children from those of intellectually disabled children at better than chance.
- **The Barnum (Forer) effect.** People rate vague, flattering, double-sided statements as uniquely accurate. Many lines in any reading, this one included, are Barnum-shaped: "serious early, blossoming later", "a reserved self with a visible image".

**What the report is, then:** an accurate account of what *the traditions say* about this sky, computed correctly. It is not a validated forecast. Its best uses are reflective: a vocabulary for strengths, a calendar for planned reviews, a prompt for self-examination. Its worst use is fear, for example buying remedies against doshas that, as Part A shows, mostly aren't even present.

**Confidence in this answer:** high.

## B13. "Which school should I trust when they disagree?"

A practical hierarchy, using the method of this whole report:

1. **Trust convergence over any single school.** Where Parashari, Jaimini, Nadi, ashtakavarga and the Western reading agree, the claim is as solid as astrology gets. Examples: the slow-build Saturn arc; Venus as engine; 2029–32 as the peak window.
2. **Trust robust factors over fragile ones.** Sign-based layers (Nadi, Jaimini padas and Chara dasha, ashtakavarga, Yogini dasha) survive birth-time error. KP sub-lords, D9 and D10 lagnas, and the D7, D12 and D60 charts don't.
3. **Use each school for what it is built for.**
   - Parashari: structure and yogas.
   - Jaimini: status, image and sign-based timing.
   - Nadi: the life story without the clock.
   - KP: yes/no event questions, but only *after* rectification.
   - Western: psychological texture, in its own tropical frame. Don't mix the zodiacs.
4. **Let your own dated events adjudicate.** Three to five well-dated events (ch. 04 §4.6) will show which candidate time, and so which fragile reading, fits. That is the one empirical test available to an individual.
5. **Be most suspicious where a school's answer would sell a remedy.**
