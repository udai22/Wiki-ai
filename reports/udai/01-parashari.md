# 1. The Parashari reading

*School: classical Parashari. Sources are BPHS (Santhanam numbering), Varahamihira's* Brihat Jataka *(BJ), Kalyana Varma's* Saravali*, Mantreswara's* Phaladeepika *(PD), Vaidyanatha's* Jataka Parijata *(JP),* Uttara Kalamrita *(UK), and the modern interpreters B.V. Raman and K.N. Rao. Chapter numbers differ between recensions. Where I am unsure of a number I name the chapter's topic instead and say so.*

Chart: Capricorn lagna 11°29′ (Shravana pada 1), Lahiri, whole-sign houses. Positions are from the data pack.

## 1.0 How far each claim can be trusted

The birth time may be off by a few minutes either way, so each claim below carries one of three tags.

| Tag | What it rests on | How far the birth time can move |
|---|---|---|

| **[Time-sensitive]** | The D9 lagna, the D10 lagna, and anything counted from them | D9 is **Aries only from −5.5 to +7 min**: Pisces from −18 to −5.5, Taurus from +7 to +19. D10 is **Scorpio (−20 to −9), Sagittarius (−9 to +2), Capricorn (+2 to +13)**, then Aquarius |

So everything in §1.1–1.4 comes from the rasi chart and holds across the whole plausible window. Only §1.5 depends on a precise birth time.

## 1.1 The lagna and its lord: Saturn deeply debilitated, then cancelled

**The facts [Robust].** Saturn rules the 1st (Capricorn) and the 2nd (Aquarius). It sits in the 4th, in Aries, at **19°34′**. Its deepest debilitation point (*parama nīca*) is Aries 20°, so Saturn is within half a degree of it, as deep as debilitation gets. It is direct, not retrograde, so *Saravali*'s idea that a retrograde planet gains strength does not apply. Jupiter shares the sign at 5°12′. Mars aspects Saturn by its 7th aspect from Libra, and Saturn aspects Mars back. Saturn's own 10th aspect falls on Capricorn, so **the lagna lord aspects its own lagna**. BPHS's house-judgement chapters (ch. 11 ff.) count this as protecting the house.

**Neecha-bhanga, rule by rule.** The fullest lists come from PD ch. 7 (roughly śl. 26–30) and JP ch. 7, which Raman follows in *Three Hundred Important Combinations*. BPHS's own statement is brief, and its place in the text varies by edition.

| # | Condition (source) | This chart | Applies? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lord of the debilitation sign in a kendra from the lagna (PD 7) | Mars, lord of Aries, is in the **10th** | **Yes** |
| 2 | Same lord in a kendra from the Moon | Mars is 2nd from the Virgo Moon | No |
| 3 | Lord of the planet's exaltation sign in a kendra from the lagna (PD 7) | Venus, lord of Libra, is in the **7th** | **Yes** |
| 4 | Same lord in a kendra from the Moon | Venus is 11th from the Moon | No |
| 5 | The planet that is exalted in the debilitation sign, in a kendra from the lagna or Moon (PD 7: Sun exalts in Aries) | Sun is 6th from the lagna but **10th from the Moon** | **Yes, from the Moon** |
| 6 | Debilitation-sign lord and exaltation-sign lord in mutual kendras (cited in JP and in Raman) | Venus in Cancer and Mars in Libra are 4th/10th to each other | **Yes** |
| 7 | Debilitated planet aspected by the lord of its sign (in several lists, including Raman's) | Mars aspects Saturn | **Yes** |
| 8 | Debilitated planet exalted in navamsa | Saturn is in Virgo in D9 | No |
| 9 | Lord of the planet's navamsa sign in a kendra or trikona (in some later lists; how authoritative it is is uncertain) | Mercury, lord of Virgo, is in the 7th | Yes (minor rule) |
| 10 | Debilitated planet joined by an exalted planet | Jupiter is not exalted in Aries | No |
| 11 | Debilitated planet retrograde (*Saravali* on *cheṣṭā*) | Direct | No |

Jupiter's conjunction does not formally cancel the debilitation under any classical rule. The texts do describe Jupiter's company as softening (*Saravali*, on two-planet combinations). Here, though, Jupiter is the functional lord of the 3rd and 12th for a Capricorn lagna, which BPHS ch. 34 counts among the functional malefics. So the softening is real but not clean.

**Is the result a raja yoga, or just a cancellation?** The masters split here.

- **PD 7** names the outcome *neecha-bhanga raja yoga* and gives it royal results.
- **BJ** has no developed neecha-bhanga doctrine. Its raja yogas (ch. 11) rest on exalted planets, so the strict Varahamihira view grants no special yoga.
- A common modern reading, widely associated with **Raman**, is that cancellation turns weakness into strength **after an early setback**. It does not erase the debilitation.
- **K.N. Rao** is often cited as saying the same thing more firmly. He holds that cancellation gives results mainly in the planet's own dasha or antardasha, that it must be confirmed in the navamsa, and that its typical story is a rise that comes after a fall.

The D9 check is mixed [Robust]: Saturn's navamsa sign is Virgo, a friendly sign, neither exalted nor debilitated. The D10 check is notably strong [Robust]: **Saturn is exalted in the D10 (Libra)**, together with Venus in its own sign and Mars.

**Synthesis.** The texts associate this pattern with a life lord who matures slowly. Early on, Saturn's themes may feel thin or delayed: steadiness, family resources (2nd lord), home and inner security (4th house). Over time the person builds an unusually well-earned competence. Five or six listed cancellation conditions apply, but they are not independent: they rest on three underlying placements (Mars in the 10th, Venus in the 7th, the Sun 10th from the Moon), plus the minor rule 9. Rule 6 follows automatically from Mars and Venus both being in lagna kendras, and rule 7 follows from Mars in Libra, which always aspects Aries. By the rules this is still a clear case of neecha-bhanga, but it is three confirmations, not six. It is still debilitation that has been cancelled, not exaltation. Saturn's own Vimshottari period begins around **Oct 2037** (±weeks, [Moderate]). That is when Rao's rule says to look for the cancellation's full results.

## 1.2 The 7th–9th–10th complex and the remaining planets

**Venus as yogakaraka [Robust].** For a Capricorn lagna, Venus rules the 5th (a trikona) and the 10th (a kendra). BPHS ch. 34 makes it the single yogakaraka for this lagna. Venus sits in the 7th, a kendra, with:

- **Mercury**, lord of the 9th and 6th. This gives two links: the 9th lord with the 10th lord, a **dharma-karmadhipati yoga** (BPHS ch. 39/41, raja yogas), and the 5th lord with the 9th lord, a trikona-lords link.
- **Rahu**, at 20°05′ Cancer, only **1°44′ from Venus** (21°49′). Both are in Ashlesha pada 2.

Neither Venus nor Mercury is combust. Venus is 45° from the Sun and Mercury 24.5°. All three planets in the 7th aspect the lagna, so the yogakaraka directly sees the lagna. Mercury also rules the 6th, which slightly mixes its contribution. The raja yoga runs mainly through Mercury's 9th-house lordship.

**Parivartana [Robust].** The Moon (7th lord) is in Virgo, Mercury's sign. Mercury is in Cancer, the Moon's sign. This is an **exchange between the 7th and 9th lords**. The Moon-ruled sign here is the 7th and the Mercury-ruled sign is the 9th, so the 6th house plays no part. Under PD ch. 6's three classes of exchange, this is a **Maha parivartana**, the auspicious kind. It is the chart's most overlooked raja-yoga link: dharma (9th) and partnership (7th) feed each other, and through the exchange the Moon is tied into the Venus–Mercury group as well.

**Does Rahu spoil the Venus yoga?** The traditions disagree.

- *For "enhances":* BPHS (ch. 34 on yogakarakas) says a node in a kendra or trikona, associated with a kendra or trikona lord, itself becomes a yoga-giver. Rahu meets this exactly: it is in a kendra and joined with the lord of the 5th and 10th. The maxim *śanivad rāhu* ("Rahu acts like Saturn") also points to Saturn, the lagna lord, so Rahu here behaves somewhat like the lagna lord.
- *For "modifies or obscures":* a widely taught view, common in Raman's practical writings, holds that a node within a few degrees of a yoga planet takes over its significations. The results come in ways that are unconventional, foreign, or larger than expected, and are not always smooth. A 1°44′ conjunction is about as tight as this gets.
- *A fair reading:* the yoga stands, and Rahu changes its texture rather than cancelling it. The Rahu mahadasha (2003–2021, already past) and the coming **Jupiter–Venus** antardasha (about Aug 2029–Apr 2032, [Moderate]) are the periods in which tradition would weigh this combination.

**Mars in the 10th [Robust].** Mars has full **dig bala** (directional strength) in the 10th. It is **vargottama** (Libra in both D1 and D9), and it sits in **Chitra, its own nakshatra**. It is *not* **Ruchaka** yoga, though: BJ ch. 13 requires own or exaltation sign, and Libra is Venus's. Call it "Ruchaka-like strength" without the yoga. Functionally, Mars rules the 4th and 11th and is listed among the malefics for Capricorn (BPHS ch. 34). So this is a forceful, capable 10th-house planet whose drive is tied to gains and to home affairs. Jupiter and Saturn both aspect it. Tradition associates the Mars–Saturn mutual aspect across the 4–10 axis with pressure between home and career. The same aspect is condition 7 of the neecha-bhanga, so the source of friction is also the source of cancellation.

**Jupiter–Saturn in the 4th [Robust].** Jupiter is in a kendra but in Aries, which is not its own or exaltation sign, so there is no Hamsa yoga. Saturn is debilitated, so no Shasha. Jupiter's 5th, 7th and 9th aspects fall on the 8th, 10th and 12th. Jupiter is the current mahadasha lord (2021–2037).

**Moon in the 9th, Chitra [Robust].** The Moon is at 28°32′ Virgo, waxing (111.6° from the Sun, about the 10th tithi), and vargottama. It is also the **Atmakaraka**, the planet with the highest degree. A bright, vargottama 7th lord in the 9th, joined to Mercury by exchange, is among the chart's better-placed factors. The 9th house's low ashtakavarga score qualifies this (see §1.3).

**Sun in the 6th [Robust].** The 8th lord sits in the 6th, a dusthana, which forms **Sarala yoga**, a form of Viparita raja yoga (UK; PD ch. 6). A Sun in the 6th is traditionally read as competitive strength. Saturn's 3rd aspect falls on it.

**Ketu in the lagna [Robust].** Ketu is at 20°05′ Capricorn in Shravana, 8½° past the ascendant degree. The lagna receives aspects from Saturn (its own lord), Venus, Mercury and Mars. By disputed node aspects (see below) it also receives one from Rahu. The texts associate a lagna Ketu with an inward, detached, searching temperament. The yogakaraka and the lagna lord both aspect the lagna, which steadies it.

## 1.3 Ashtakavarga [Robust]

The SAV total is the fixed 337, an average of about 28 per sign. The texts (BPHS ch. 66–72; PD ch. 23–24 on ashtakavarga) treat more than 28 as supportive and fewer than 25 as weak.

- **Scorpio (11th) = 38**, by far the strongest sign. It is the house of gains, networks and aspirations. Several BAVs peak here: Moon 7, Mercury 7, Mars 6, Venus 6. Its lord Mars is the strong 10th-house planet. The common rule **11th > 12th** (38 vs 26) holds. The more refined **11th > 10th > 1st** fails, because the 10th (25) is below the lagna (27). Tradition reads this as a chart where effort turns into gains.
- **Leo (8th) = 24** and **Virgo (9th) = 24**, the two lowest. A low 8th is not alarming in itself. Some authors even prefer a modest 8th. A low **9th** is the real nuance: the Moon, the Atmakaraka, sits in a sign with below-average support. Ashtakavarga logic says the 9th house's themes (teachers, fortune, higher learning, the father's line) come through effort and timing rather than as a given. It also says transits through Virgo, such as Saturn's next passage there at the end of the 2030s, count for less.
- **Kendras:** lagna 27, 4th 31, 7th 28, 10th 25. The 4th, home of the debilitated lagna lord, scores above average, a quiet support for §1.1.
- **Bindus in the planets' own BAVs:** Jupiter has 6 in Aries (strong). Saturn has only 3 in Aries, and just **1 in Libra**, its exaltation sign and the 10th house. Venus has 3 in Cancer, Mars 4 in Libra, and the Sun 6 in Gemini.
- *Caveat:* these are raw scores. Trikona and ekadhipatya shodhana (the reductions) were not applied, and BPHS uses those for its finer calculations.

## 1.4 Where the masters disagree, in summary

1. **Node aspects.** Some recensions of BPHS, and a large modern lineage including the circles associated with K.N. Rao, give Rahu and Ketu Jupiter-like 5th/7th/9th aspects. Others, including much of Raman's practice, accept only the 7th or none at all. Under 5/7/9, Rahu aspects the 11th, the 1st and the 3rd, and Ketu aspects Venus, Mercury and the Moon. Under the stricter view, only the 7th-axis contact remains. This report treats node aspects as secondary.
2. **Rahu with Venus**: enhancement under the BPHS node-yogakaraka rule, or eclipse under the practitioners' conjunction rule (§1.2).
3. **Neecha-bhanga as raja yoga**: PD and JP say yes, BJ is silent, and Raman and Rao say yes with delay and conditions (§1.1).
4. **Saravali versus BPHS on sign results.** BJ (planets-in-signs chapters) and *Saravali* give Saturn in Mars's signs a terse, austere description. BPHS is more structural and weighs lordship and yoga above sign descriptions. Modern Parashari practice follows BPHS and treats the sign descriptions as modified by everything above.
5. **Functional status of Jupiter and Mars** for Capricorn (BPHS ch. 34). Strict Parashari practice lists both as functional malefics, despite Mars's dig bala and Jupiter's natural benevolence. Rao in particular insists that lordship comes first.

## 1.5 Navamsa and Dasamsa under each candidate lagna

**What holds whatever the D9 lagna [Robust].** The Moon (Virgo), Mars (Libra) and Mercury (Cancer) are vargottama. Saturn is in Virgo, which is neither exalted nor debilitated. Venus is in Capricorn, a friend's sign, with Rahu. Jupiter is in Taurus and the Sun in Sagittarius. The **Venus–Rahu pairing repeats in D9**, which reinforces §1.2's point that Rahu shapes the Venus themes.

| | **Pisces D9** (−18 to −5.5 min) | **Aries D9** (−5.5 to +7, recorded time) | **Taurus D9** (+7 to +19 min) |
|---|---|---|---|
| D9 lagna lord | Jupiter, in the 3rd | Mars, in the 7th, vargottama, aspecting the D9 lagna | Venus, in the **9th**, a trikona |
| D9 7th house and lord | Virgo: Moon + Saturn in the 7th; lord Mercury in the 5th | Libra: Mars in the 7th; lord Venus in the 10th with Rahu | Scorpio is empty; lord Mars in the 6th |
| Saturn | 7th | 6th, an upachaya | 5th. Saturn is yogakaraka for Taurus, a strong position for the lagna lord's navamsa |
| Jupiter | 3rd | 2nd | **In the D9 lagna** |
| Overall tone (per the texts) | Partnership shaped by Moon–Saturn: steady, serious, possibly later | Partnership marked by Martian directness, career through Venus–Rahu | The cleanest D9: benefic lagna, yogakaraka in a trikona |

How the conclusions move: the *Taurus* window gives the neecha-bhanga its best navamsa support, through Saturn as yogakaraka in the 5th. The *Pisces* window gives the weakest, with Saturn and the Moon in the 7th and the lagna lord in the 3rd. Mid-window, at the recorded time, the support is moderate. Anything a reading says about the D9 7th house or its lord should be tested against life events before it is relied on [Time-sensitive].

**D10 [Time-sensitive for houses; Robust for signs].** Mars, Venus (in its own sign) and Saturn (exalted) all fall in **Libra** in D10, whatever the D10 lagna.

- **Scorpio D10** (−20 to −9 min): the Libra group falls in the 12th, and the Sun in Leo is in the 10th. Career would be read through the Sun's authority, with the strong group working behind the scenes or abroad.
- **Sagittarius D10** (−9 to +2, recorded time): the Libra group is in the 11th, the house of gains. The D10 lagna lord Jupiter is in the 6th, and the 10th lord Mercury is in the 4th with Ketu.
- **Capricorn D10** (+2 to +13): the Libra group, including **exalted Saturn as D10 lagna lord, is in the D10 10th**. This is the strongest career dasamsa of the three, and it mirrors the D1 lagna.

Two cautions. The D10 lagna turns over about every 11–13 minutes, so it is the least reliable layer here. And the Sagittarius window is narrow (−9 to +2 min), so the Capricorn variant is a live possibility if the time was recorded slightly early. A rectification pass, using event dates against the Jupiter mahadasha antardashas, would be the classical way to decide.

### Key findings

- **Robust core:** Capricorn lagna, Saturn as lagna lord at 19°34′ Aries (within 0.5° of deepest debilitation), Venus as yogakaraka in the 7th with Mercury and a tight Rahu, Mars with dig bala and vargottama in the 10th. None of this changes within −43 to +66 min.
- **Neecha-bhanga is clearly supported:** 5–6 listed conditions apply (Mars and Venus in lagna kendras, Sun 10th from the Moon, Mars–Venus in mutual kendras, Mars aspecting Saturn, navamsa lord Mercury in a kendra). They are not independent: three placements (Mars in the 10th, Venus in the 7th, the Sun 10th from the Moon) generate all but the minor navamsa-lord rule. Saturn's exaltation in D10 is separate varga support, not a cancellation rule. The texts disagree on whether this is a full raja yoga. Raman and Rao read it as strength reached after early delay, delivered most fully in Saturn's own period from around 2037.
- **Real yogas:** dharma-karmadhipati (Venus–Mercury), Maha parivartana between the Moon and Mercury (7th–9th lords, often overlooked), Sarala (Sun in the 6th), Sunapha, Vesi, Pasa. Sakata (Phaladeepika form) is present but mild and mitigated. **Absent though often claimed:** Gaja Kesari (Jupiter is 8th from the Moon), Ruchaka and all the other Mahapurusha yogas, Amala, and Kemadruma (which, being adverse, it is good to rule out).
- **Ashtakavarga:** the 11th house (Scorpio) scores 38, the chart's standout gains house, and 11th > 12th holds. The 9th (Virgo, the Moon's sign) and 8th score only 24, so 9th-house fortune is a matter of effort and timing.
- **Time-sensitive layer:** the D9 lagna (Pisces, Aries or Taurus) changes the reading of partnership and how much the navamsa supports Saturn, with Taurus the most supportive and Pisces the least. The D10 lagna (Scorpio, Sagittarius or Capricorn) moves the strong Libra group (Saturn exalted, Venus in its own sign, Mars) between the 12th, 11th and 10th. A Capricorn D10, from about +2 to +13 min, would be the strongest career signature and is worth testing in rectification.
