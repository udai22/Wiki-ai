# 12 — Alexander Chizhevsky (Tchijevsky) and Heliobiology

Alexander Leonidovich Chizhevsky (Александр Леонидович Чижевский), spelled **Tchijevsky** in French-derived transliteration.
Born **7 February 1897** (26 Jan O.S.) in Tsekhanovets (Ciechanowiec, now Poland, then the Russian Empire). Died **20 December 1964**, Moscow.

He is usually called the founder of **heliobiology**, the study of how solar activity affects living things, and a pioneer of **aeroionification** (biological effects of air ions). He was also a poet and painter and a friend of Konstantin Tsiolkovsky in Kaluga. He is part of the **Russian Cosmism** current.

## 1. Life in brief

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1913–15 | Teenage observations of sunspots in Kaluga; begins the solar–history idea |
| 1915 | Studies at the Moscow Commercial Institute and the Moscow Archaeological Institute; serves briefly at the WWI front |
| 1917–18 | Defends a dissertation on the periodicity of the world-historical process (reported, in Moscow) |
| 1918–1920s | Air-ion experiments on animals; works in Kaluga, then Moscow |
| **1924** | ***Physical Factors of the Historical Process*** (*Физические факторы исторического процесса*), Kaluga brochure (printed reportedly with support from Commissar Lunacharsky) |
| 1920s–30s | Attacked in the Soviet press (notably a 1935 *Pravda* article) as a "pseudo-scientist" and "counter-revolutionary"; the theory clashed with Marxist historical materialism |
| 1931 | Leads a central laboratory for ionification under the People's Commissariat for Agriculture |
| 1939 | Named honorary president of the First International Congress of Biophysics (New York) *in absentia*. Often said to have been nominated for the Nobel Prize; this is **frequently repeated but poorly documented** |
| **Jan 1942** | **Arrested** on denunciation, sentenced to **8 years**. Labour camp in the **northern Urals** (Ivdellag), later transferred to Karlag (Karaganda region). Continued research on blood (erythrocyte "structural analysis") in camp labs |
| 1950 | Released into exile in **Karaganda, Kazakhstan** (some sources give later dates); works in local medical labs |
| 1958 | Allowed to return to Moscow |
| 1962 | Rehabilitated |
| 1964 | Dies in Moscow |
| 1973 | Posthumous major book: ***The Terrestrial Echo of Solar Storms*** (*Земное эхо солнечных бурь*, Moscow: Mysl') |

The 1989 Soviet film ***Sun Captive*** (*Пленник солнца*) dramatised his life. Kaluga hosts a Chizhevsky house-museum.

## 2. The historiometric theory

### Data
- He compiled "mass events" (wars, revolutions, uprisings, mass migrations, epidemics, religious movements) for **Russia and 71 other countries**, i.e. **72 countries**, from **500 BCE to 1922 CE**.
- He compared them with a solar-activity series. Before telescopic observation (1610) this came from **naked-eye sunspot and aurora records** and reconstructions, which is a weak point.

### The 11-year cycle in four phases
In his scheme, each ~11-year sunspot cycle maps to a cycle of collective excitability:

| Phase | Approx. length | Solar state | Social character (Chizhevsky) | Share of mass events (as commonly cited*) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Minimum excitability | ~3 years | Sunspot minimum | Masses passive, indifferent to politics; peace, autocracy or rule by a minority; "tolerance" | ~5% |
| 2. Growing excitability | ~2 years | Rising activity | New ideas, organisation, new leaders appear; masses begin to coalesce | ~20% |
| 3. Maximum excitability | ~3 years | Sunspot maximum | Revolutions, wars, uprisings, mass migrations; crowd psychology dominates | ~60% |
| 4. Decreasing excitability | ~3 years | Declining activity | Fatigue, apathy; masses demand peace; consolidation | ~15% |

\*The percentage split is widely repeated in secondary literature. Dewey (1971) reproduces the four phases and their lengths. Verify the exact percentages against the 1924 text before quoting them as Chizhevsky's.

He also built a **"mass excitability index"** (index of "psychic excitability" of the masses) from the yearly count and intensity of mass events and plotted it against sunspot numbers. His charts show the two curves broadly in step over centuries.

### Proposed mechanism
- He did not suggest astrology-style symbolism. He held that **electromagnetic and corpuscular radiation** from active-Sun periods acts on the **nervous system**, raising collective irritability, and that crowds amplify individual excitability.
- Elsewhere he linked solar activity to **epidemics** (cholera, plague, diphtheria), notably in *Epidemic Catastrophes and the Periodic Activity of the Sun* (1930). He also linked it to biological populations (locusts), mortality and later blood-sedimentation properties.
- His stated aim was to place history inside natural science: "the life of humanity is part of the life of the cosmos".

## 3. Air ionisation: the "Chizhevsky chandelier"

- From around 1919 he exposed animals to **negatively charged air ions** and reported better health, growth and lifespan. He reported harm from positive ions or ion-depleted air.
- He invented the **"Chizhevsky chandelier"** (люстра Чижевского): a high-voltage electrode array, a ring or chandelier of needle points at tens of kV, that produces negative ions by corona discharge. Mass-market versions are still sold in Russia and elsewhere.
- **Status:** later research on negative air ions finds **small or inconsistent** effects on most outcomes. There is reasonable evidence for a modest antidepressant effect at **high densities** (e.g. Terman's light/ion studies for seasonal affective disorder; Perez et al. 2013 meta-analysis). Claims of broad health and longevity benefits are not established. Corona-discharge ionisers can also produce **ozone**.
- Note: some popular summaries say negative ions cause "mass excitability". That conflates two of his separate research lines. The excitability theory is about **solar activity**.

## 4. Dewey and the West
- Chizhevsky presented his work in French (e.g. *Les épidémies et les perturbations électromagnétiques du milieu extérieur*, 1938) and was known to European climatologists.
- An abridged English translation of the 1924 work, *"Physical factors of the historical process"*, appeared in the FSC journal **Cycles (1971)**. **Edward Dewey's** 1971 popular book spread the four-phase scheme in the West, and it is the main source of the Anglophone "Tchijevsky" legend. Dewey added the observation that in Chizhevsky's data the sunspot peak tends to **precede** the excitability peak by about a year. See [11-edward-dewey-cycles.md](11-edward-dewey-cycles.md).

## 5. Later research

### Chronobiology: Franz Halberg
**Franz Halberg (1919–2013)**, University of Minnesota, founded modern **chronobiology** and coined "**circadian**" (1959). From the 1980s–2000s he worked with Russian and other colleagues (e.g. Cornélissen, Breus) on **"chronomics"**. They reported non-photic, about-weekly (circaseptan), about-yearly and about-decadal (~10.5 yr) components in blood pressure and heart rate and suggested solar and geomagnetic influences on them. Halberg explicitly honoured Chizhevsky. These findings come largely from a single network of researchers and are **not widely replicated** outside it.

### Ertel's re-analysis
**Suitbert Ertel** (Göttingen psychologist; also known for his re-analysis of Gauquelin) tested Chizhevsky's claim with **independent historical compilations**, building a master index of "violence from below" (revolts, revolutions) for roughly **1700–1985**. He reported a **substantial positive relationship** between solar activity and revolutionary activity, broadly supporting Chizhevsky. The paper is: *"Space weather and revolutions: Chizhevsky's heliobiological claim scrutinized"*, *Studia Psychologica* 39 (1997, often cited as 1996). It has had limited independent follow-up. Ertel's work in other areas (Gauquelin) was also controversial.

### Other studies
- **Putilov (1992)** tested Chizhevsky using Soviet historical reference works and reported partial support.
- **Mikulecký and others** (e.g. a 2007 *Neuroendocrinology Letters* paper) reported revolutions clustering near solar maxima and cultural "primes" near minima, while calling the mechanism unproven.
- **Russian heliobiology** (Breus, Vladimirsky, Zelenyi and others) continues. Reviews such as Breus et al. 2017 in *Herald of the Russian Academy of Sciences* claim his correlations have been independently confirmed. These authors are advocates of the field.
- **Geomagnetic activity and health.** There is a large, mixed literature: myocardial infarction and stroke incidence, blood pressure, heart-rate variability, psychiatric admissions and suicide. Several studies report modest associations with geomagnetic storms (Kp/Ap indices). Others find none. Publication bias and multiple testing are serious concerns. Some HRV and melatonin findings are suggestive. No consensus mechanism exists.
- **Space weather** is now a real engineering discipline: it affects power grids, satellites, radio and aviation radiation dose. This supports the general idea that the "Sun affects Earth's technological environment" but says nothing directly about the historical claim.

## 6. Critiques
1. **Coding subjectivity.** What counts as a "mass event", how intense it is, and how to date its start are judgement calls. Chizhevsky coded the data knowing the hypothesis, with no blinding.
2. **Pre-telescopic solar data.** For about 2,000 of the 2,400 years, solar activity is reconstructed from sparse sunspot and aurora reports. Those records are themselves affected by social conditions such as chronicle density and literacy.
3. **Base-rate problem.** A "maximum" window of ~3–5 years covers about a third to a half of the cycle, so a lot of clustering happens by chance. The cycle also varies from about 9 to 14 years, which leaves room to choose windows after the fact.
4. **No stable lag.** Peaks sometimes lead and sometimes lag. Dewey noted the sunspot peak precedes the index.
5. **Political suppression** cut short Soviet testing. Some sympathetic narratives treat persecution as evidence of truth, which it is not.
6. **Modern status:** the solar–revolution link is **not accepted** in mainstream history or social science. It survives as a niche hypothesis with some supportive re-analyses (Ertel) and no decisive, preregistered, independent test.

## 7. Relevance to this project
- It is the cleanest **physically framed** version of mundane astrology: a measurable sky variable (sunspot number, F10.7 flux, Kp/Ap) set against measurable social series.
- It is easy to test with the engine. Take sunspot numbers (SILSO, monthly since 1749) and a conflict dataset (UCDP, COW, or Ertel-style indices). Run cross-correlation with lags and a Chizhevsky-phase classification. Use surrogate tests (phase-randomised sunspot series) and a **pre-specified** lag window.
- Solar maximum timing comes from SILSO. Cycle 25 peaked around late 2024 (provisional).
