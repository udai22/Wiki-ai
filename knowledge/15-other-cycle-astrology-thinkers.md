# 15 — Other Cycle and Astrology Thinkers: Short Entries

Brief entries in rough chronological order, grouped as **(A) astrologers and philosophers**, **(B) mundane and financial astrology**, **(C) economic and social cycle theorists**, **(D) solar and biological cycle science**. Each entry ends with a one-line *status* note. For the synthesis map see [10-cycles-thinkers.md](10-cycles-thinkers.md).

## A. Astrologers, astronomers and philosophers

### Claudius Ptolemy (c. 100–170 CE)
- ***Tetrabiblos*** ("Four Books", Greek *Apotelesmatika*), the foundational textbook of Western astrology, alongside his astronomical *Almagest*.
- Gives a **naturalistic** rationale: the heavens act physically (heat, moisture) on the sublunary world. The Sun's seasons and the Moon's tides are the model cases. Astrology is a *conjectural* art, less certain than astronomy.
- Book II covers **mundane/universal astrology** (eclipses, countries, weather), the part that precedes individual nativities (Books III–IV).
- The pseudo-Ptolemaic *Centiloquium* is the source of "the wise man rules the stars".
- *Status:* historically central. Its physics is obsolete.

### Abu Ma'shar al-Balkhi (Albumasar, 787–886)
- Persian astrologer in Baghdad. ***Kitāb al-qirānāt*** (*On the Great Conjunctions / Book of Religions and Dynasties*) and the *Introductorium maius*.
- **Jupiter–Saturn conjunction theory.** The two meet every **~19.86 years** (the "great conjunction"). Successive conjunctions stay in one elemental **triplicity** for about 200–240 years, then shift to the next ("mutation"). The full return through all four triplicities takes **~960 years**. These levels were read as timing for changes of dynasty, religion and prophets.
- Hugely influential in Latin Europe (Roger Bacon, Pierre d'Ailly, Kepler's *De stella nova*).
- *Status:* the prototype of planetary-cycle history. The J–S period is a real astronomical constant; its historical correlations are untested in a controlled sense.

### Nostradamus (Michel de Nostredame, 1503–1566)
- French apothecary and astrologer. ***Les Prophéties*** (1555; expanded 1557/1568), rhymed quatrains grouped in "centuries" of 100. He also produced annual almanacs.
- Used astrology (including conjunction theory) plus older prophetic sources. The quatrains are vague and undated, so they are retrofitted after events.
- *Status:* no demonstrated predictive success. A classic case of post-hoc interpretation.

### Johannes Kepler (1571–1630)
- Imperial mathematician. He cast horoscopes professionally and was sceptical of most traditional rules.
- ***De fundamentis astrologiae certioribus*** (1601), ***De stella nova*** (1606, nova and great conjunction), ***Tertius interveniens*** (1610, defends a reformed astrology: "don't throw out the baby with the bathwater").
- ***Harmonices Mundi*** (1619). Contains the **third law of planetary motion**. It treats **aspects** as geometric **harmonics**, consonances of the circle that the soul of Earth and humans "resonate" with. He kept the conjunction, opposition, trine, square and sextile, and added the **quintile (72°), biquintile (144°) and sesquiquadrate (135°)**, deriving aspects from regular polygons rather than from signs.
- *Status:* he rejected zodiac signs and houses as human conventions. His aspect-as-resonance idea leads to harmonic astrology (John Addey) and is conceptually parallel to Dewey/Tomes harmonics.

### Carl Gustav Jung (1875–1961)
- Swiss psychiatrist. He used horoscopes in analysis as a map of psychic dispositions. He read astrology as **projection** of the collective unconscious onto the heavens, and alchemy similarly (see his Paracelsus essays, [13-paracelsus.md](13-paracelsus.md#6-later-influence)).
- **Synchronicity**: "an acausal connecting principle", meaning meaningful coincidence without a causal link. Published 1952 with Wolfgang Pauli (*Naturerklärung und Psyche*; English 1955).
- **The astrological marriage experiment.** In *Synchronicity* he compared Sun/Moon/Ascendant contacts (e.g. Moon conjunct Sun, Moon conjunct Moon) in the charts of **483 married couples**, analysed in three batches (180, 220 and 83 couples), against unmarried random pairings. Each batch "confirmed" a different traditional marriage aspect, and the frequencies were not statistically significant overall. Jung read the pattern as synchronistic, partly reflecting the experimenters' expectations. A later replication by Dean and colleagues with ~960 couples found **no** effect.
- *Status:* synchronicity is a philosophical interpretation, not a testable mechanism. Jung's own data are chance-level.

### Dane Rudhyar (1895–1985)
- French-American composer and astrologer. ***The Astrology of Personality*** (1936) launched **humanistic** (later "transpersonal") astrology, synthesising Jung, holism and Theosophy.
- Developed **cycle-based interpretation**: the **lunation cycle** (*The Lunation Cycle*, 1967; eight phases of the Sun–Moon cycle as personality types), planetary cycles as phases of growth, and the **"astrology of history"**.
- *Status:* interpretive framework; it makes no empirical claims.

### Michel Gauquelin (1928–1991)
- French psychologist and statistician. ***L'Influence des astres*** (1955), later *The Cosmic Clocks* (1967) and others.
- Analysed tens of thousands of birth records. Most traditional astrology (signs, aspects) **failed**. But **Mars** occurred more often than expected in the **"key sectors"** (just after rising and just after upper culmination) in the charts of eminent **sports champions** (the "**Mars effect**"). He reported similar effects for Jupiter with actors/military leaders, Saturn with scientists and the Moon with writers. He also reported a heredity effect.
- Controversy: replication attempts by the Belgian **Comité Para** (1976, replicated it, then disputed it), CSICOP's US test (1979–80, negative; marred by internal dispute) and the French **CFEPP** (1990s, published 2000, negative). **Ertel** argued for an eminence gradient that supported Gauquelin. Critics (e.g. Benski et al.; Nienhuys) argued that **selection bias** in choosing "eminent" champions explained it. Gauquelin's data are publicly archived.
- *Status:* the strongest-looking statistical astrology claim, **unresolved but widely regarded as an artifact**. It concerns planetary *diurnal position*, not traditional astrology.

### Richard Tarnas (b. 1950)
- American cultural historian (CIIS). *The Passion of the Western Mind* (1991); ***Cosmos and Psyche: Intimations of a New World View*** (2006).
- **Archetypal astrology** of history. Correlates **outer-planet cycles** with cultural periods: **Uranus–Pluto** (the 1960s, 1789–99, 1840s revolutions), **Saturn–Pluto** (wars, crises, contraction: 1914, 1939–47, 2001, 2020), **Jupiter–Uranus** (breakthroughs), **Saturn–Neptune** (dissolution of structures, e.g. 1989–91). Uses wide orbs (~15–20° for conjunctions/oppositions).
- Collaborators: Stanislav Grof (holotropic states), and the *Archai* journal.
- *Status:* no statistical controls. Wide orbs, many aspect types and flexible historical selection make it hard to falsify. Valuable as **hypotheses** for the engine (e.g. test conflict-onset rates in Saturn–Pluto windows against base rates).

### André Barbault (1921–2019)
- French astrologer, central to 20th-century **mundane astrology**. *Les astres et l'histoire* (1967), *Le pronostic expérimental en astrologie* (1973), *Défense et illustration de l'astrologie* (1955).
- Popularised the **cyclic index** (*indice cyclique*), first proposed by **Henri Gouchon** in the 1940s. For each year, sum the angular distances between every pair of the five slowest bodies (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Pluto: 10 pairs, each 0–180°). **Low values** (planets bunched) were associated with crises and wars (troughs around 1914–18, 1940–45, 1982–83, 2020). High values were associated with expansion and peace. A related "planetary concentration index" uses the same principle.
- He is credited by followers with forecasting a major Soviet crisis around 1989–90 from the Saturn–Neptune conjunction (1989). Check the original publication dates before repeating this.
- *Status:* easy to compute and test (the engine can generate it from the ephemeris). Its correlation with wars has **not** been demonstrated in a controlled, pre-registered analysis, and a few large troughs dominate it.

**Cyclic index formula:**
```
CI(t) = Σ_{i<j ∈ {J,S,U,N,P}} d(λ_i, λ_j),   d = min(|Δλ|, 360°−|Δλ|)
```

## B. Financial and mundane market astrology

### W. D. Gann (William Delbert Gann, 1878–1955)
- American trader. Developed geometric and time-cycle techniques: **Gann angles** (1×1 = 45°), the **Square of Nine**, and anniversary and seasonal dates. ***The Tunnel Thru the Air*** (1927) is a novel with coded forecasts. *Truth of the Stock Tape* (1923), *45 Years in Wall Street* (1949).
- Used natural and astronomical cycles: 20-yr and 30-yr periods (J–S and Saturn), 60-yr and 90-yr periods.
- Legends of fabulous trading accuracy (e.g. the 1909 *Ticker and Investment Digest* article) are **poorly documented**. His estate at death was modest.
- *Status:* the techniques have no demonstrated edge in independent tests. Still widely used in technical-analysis subcultures.

### Louise McWhirter
- Author of ***Theory of Stock Market Forecasting*** (1938). Almost nothing is known about her biography, and even the name may be a pen name.
- Claimed the **18.6-year cycle of the Moon's North Node** through the zodiac governs US business activity. Peaks and troughs are tied to the node's sign position, modified by outer-planet transits to the NYSE chart and by eclipses, new moons and Mars/Mercury stations.
- *Status:* the 18.6-yr lunar nodal cycle is real (it affects tides measurably). The business-cycle link is unsupported by controlled tests. It is sometimes conflated with ~18-yr real-estate cycles ([11](11-edward-dewey-cycles.md)).

### Raymond A. Merriman (b. 1946)
- American financial-cycles analyst and astrologer, founder of the **Merriman Market Analyst (MMA)** publications. Author of *The Ultimate Book on Stock Market Timing* (5 vols.) and an annual *Forecast* book.
- Combines technical **cycle analysis** (Hurst-style nested cycles, e.g. ~18-week and 4-year) with **geocosmic signatures** (planetary stations, aspects, ingresses). He reports critical reversal dates within about ±3 trading days.
- *Status:* published track records are self-assessed. No independent, controlled evaluation is known.

## C. Economic and social cycle theorists

| Theorist | Cycle | Period | Mechanism proposed | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Joseph Kitchin** (1861–1932) | Kitchin / inventory cycle | ~40 months (3–5 yr); Kitchin 1923, *Rev. Econ. Stat.* | Inventory adjustment, information lags | Short inventory fluctuations are real but irregular |
| **Clément Juglar** (1819–1905) | Juglar / fixed-investment cycle | ~7–11 yr; *Des crises commerciales* (1862) | Credit and fixed-capital investment | The classic "business cycle"; real but not strictly periodic |
| **Simon Kuznets** (1901–1985; Nobel 1971) | Kuznets swing | ~15–25 yr (1930) | Demographics, migration, construction | Debated; some argue it is a statistical artifact of filtering (Howrey 1968, Adelman) |
| **Nikolai Kondratiev** (1892–1938) | Kondratiev long wave | ~45–60 yr; *The Major Economic Cycles* (1925) | Capital renewal; later Schumpeter's technology waves | Kondratiev was arrested in 1930 and **executed in 1938** in the Stalinist purges. The long wave is contested: too few repetitions for statistical proof, and data-dependent |
| **William Strauss** (1947–2007) & **Neil Howe** (b. 1951) | Generational **saeculum** of four "turnings" | ~80–90 yr (four ~20–22-yr turnings); *Generations* (1991), *The Fourth Turning* (1997), Howe's *The Fourth Turning Is Here* (2023) | Generational archetypes (Prophet, Nomad, Hero, Artist) and social mood | Influential in pop culture and politics. Academic historians criticise it as **unfalsifiable** and US-centric. Note that ~84 yr ≈ Uranus orbit, which astrologers often point out |

Joseph Schumpeter (*Business Cycles*, 1939) tried to fit Kitchin, Juglar and Kondratiev into one nested scheme (roughly 3 Kitchins ≈ 1 Juglar, 6 Juglars ≈ 1 Kondratiev). This parallels Dewey's 2/3 harmonic table.

## D. Solar and biological cycle science

### Heinrich Schwabe (1789–1875): the sunspot cycle
- German amateur astronomer. While hunting for an intra-Mercurial planet, he counted sunspots daily from 1826 and reported a **~10-year** periodicity in 1843.
- **Rudolf Wolf** (1816–1893) set up the sunspot number (1848), reconstructed cycles back to 1749 (Cycle 1 = 1755–66), and refined the mean to **~11.1 years**. Individual cycles run about **9–14 years**.
- *Status:* fundamental, solid solar physics. Cycle 25 began December 2019 and peaked around 2024–25 (SILSO provisional).

### George Ellery Hale (1868–1938): the magnetic cycle
- Discovered magnetic fields in sunspots (1908, Zeeman effect). With Nicholson (1919/1925) he showed that sunspot **magnetic polarity reverses each cycle**. The full magnetic cycle is **~22 years** (the Hale cycle). Related laws: Hale's polarity law, Joy's law.
- Longer modulations: the **Gleissberg** cycle (~80–100 yr) and the **de Vries/Suess** cycle (~200–210 yr, from radiocarbon). Grand minima include Maunder (1645–1715) and Spörer.
- *Status:* solid. Relevant to Chizhevsky ([12](12-chizhevsky.md)) and Landscheidt.

### Theodor Landscheidt (1927–2004)
- German jurist and amateur astronomer/astrologer, founder of the Schroeter Institute. *Sun–Earth–Man: A Mesh of Cosmic Oscillations* (1989); *Astrology: Hope for a Science?*
- Extended **Paul Jose's** (1965) **solar inertial motion** idea: the Sun moves around the solar-system barycentre, driven mainly by Jupiter and Saturn, with a ~178.7-yr pattern. Landscheidt claimed that changes in the Sun's orbital angular momentum ("impulses of torque") time solar-activity extremes, El Niño, and climate. He forecast a **Gleissberg grand minimum around 2030**.
- *Status:* mixed forecasting record. The physical mechanism is **rejected by most solar physicists** because planetary tidal and torque effects on the solar interior are tiny. A minority line of research continues (Scafetta; Stefani et al. on Venus–Earth–Jupiter tidal synchronisation of the dynamo). Details in [11 §5](11-edward-dewey-cycles.md#5-planetary-period-connections).

### Franz Halberg (1919–2013): chronobiology
- Romanian-born American physiologist (University of Minnesota). Founded **chronobiology** and coined **"circadian"** (1959; from *circa diem*, about a day). Developed the **cosinor** method for fitting rhythms to biological data, which is still a standard tool and directly usable in the engine. Promoted **chronotherapy** (timing drugs to body clocks).
- Later, in **"chronomics"**, he proposed "about-weekly", about-yearly and **about-11-year (solar)** components in human physiology and honoured Chizhevsky as a forerunner.
- *Status:* circadian biology is mainstream; the 2017 Nobel Prize went to Hall, Rosbash and Young for its molecular clock. The solar-cycle chronomics claims are **not widely replicated**.

### Also worth knowing
- **Frank A. Brown Jr.** (1908–1983): lunar and geophysical rhythms in organisms. Cited by Osho ([14](14-osho.md)).
- **Giorgio Piccardi** (1895–1972): "Piccardi tests" of solar-modulated chemical reaction rates. Unreplicated.
- **Charles Elton** (1900–1991): lynx/hare and lemming cycles. Inspired Matamek (1931) and FSC.
- **John Addey** (1920–1982): *Harmonics in Astrology* (1976). Applied Fourier and harmonic analysis to birth data (after Kepler), a bridge between astrology and Dewey-style cycle statistics.
- **Geoffrey Dean et al.**: comprehensive critical reviews of empirical astrology research (e.g. *Recent Advances in Natal Astrology*, 1977; later meta-analyses). **Shawn Carlson** (1985, *Nature* 318) ran a double-blind test of astrologers matching charts to personality profiles and found chance-level performance.

## Scientific-status summary

| Strand | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Solar cycles (Schwabe, Hale, Gleissberg) | **Strong** (physics) |
| Circadian and tidal biology (Halberg, Brown partly) | **Strong** for circadian/tidal. **Weak** for cosmic external timing in humans |
| Economic cycles (Kitchin, Juglar, Kuznets, Kondratiev) | **Mixed.** Fluctuations real, strict periodicity doubtful |
| Generational theory (Strauss–Howe) | **Weak / unfalsifiable** |
| Gauquelin Mars effect | **Disputed**, likely artifact |
| Planetary cycles and history (Abu Ma'shar, Barbault, Tarnas) | **Untested or unsupported** in controlled designs |
| Financial astrology (Gann, McWhirter, Merriman) | **No independent support** |
| Natal astrology predictions (Carlson, Dean, Jung's own data) | **Not supported** in controlled tests |
| Planetary–solar coupling (Jose, Landscheidt) | **Minority hypothesis**; mechanism disputed |

The project can turn many of these into **testable hypotheses** with the ephemeris and `cycles.py` (cyclic index against conflict onset, Saturn–Pluto windows, nodal cycle against GDP, Mars sectors). Decide the tests in advance and correct for multiple comparisons; see [11 §7](11-edward-dewey-cycles.md#7-practical-recipe-a-dewey-style-cycle-analysis).
