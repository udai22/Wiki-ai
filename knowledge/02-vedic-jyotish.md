# Vedic Astrology (Jyotish): Masters, Texts and Techniques

> Part of the knowledge base. Start at [01-how-astrology-works.md](01-how-astrology-works.md). Techniques are applied to the reference chart in [03-reading-parasharas-light.md](03-reading-parasharas-light.md), and software is covered in [04-software.md](04-software.md).
>
> **On chapter numbers.** Classical works circulate in several recensions, so chapter numbers differ between editions. BPHS references here follow R. Santhanam's 2-volume English translation (Ranjan Publications), the edition most widely cited in English. If a number doesn't match your copy, search by the chapter title.

*Jyotiṣa* ("science of light") is one of the six *Vedāṅgas*, the limbs auxiliary to the Vedas. Its oldest text, the *Vedāṅga Jyotiṣa* attributed to Lagadha (c. 1st millennium BCE), is purely calendrical. Horoscopy with signs and houses entered India with Hellenistic material: the *Yavanajātaka* of Sphujidhvaja (c. 269/270 CE) versifies an earlier Greek-to-Sanskrit translation. Over the next 1,500 years Indian authors added a great deal that is their own: nakshatra-based dashas, divisional charts, Ashtakavarga, Jaimini's karaka system and thousands of yogas.

Tradition divides Jyotish into three *skandhas* (branches): **Siddhanta** (mathematical astronomy), **Samhita** (mundane astrology, omens, weather) and **Hora** (natal, horary and electional astrology).

## Part I: The classical masters and texts

### Maharishi Parāśara: *Bṛhat Parāśara Horā Śāstra* (BPHS)
- **Attribution.** Parashara is traditionally the Vedic sage and father of Vyasa. The surviving text is a compilation; scholars (e.g. David Pingree) date its present form to the medieval period, with substantial later additions. It takes the form of a dialogue between Parashara and his disciple Maitreya.
- **Why it matters.** It is the foundational encyclopedia of "Parashari" astrology, the default system in Parashara's Light, as the name says. Key topics and chapters (Santhanam):
  - ch. 3: planets: nature, dignity, exaltation and debilitation, natural friendships
  - ch. 4: signs
  - ch. 6–7: the sixteen divisional charts (*shodashavarga*) and their use
  - ch. 8: aspects of signs (*rashi drishti*)
  - ch. 11–24: judgement of the twelve houses
  - ch. 26: planetary aspects (*graha drishti*)
  - ch. 27: Shadbala (sixfold strength)
  - ch. 29–30: Arudha Lagna and Upapada
  - ch. 32: karakas (natural and chara)
  - ch. 34–41: yogakarakas, Nabhasa yogas, Chandra and Surya yogas, Raja yogas, Dhana yogas
  - ch. 45: avasthas (planetary states)
  - ch. 46 onward: dasha systems (Vimshottari, Ashtottari, Yogini, Kalachakra and about 40 others), with results of antardashas in the following chapters
  - ch. 66–72: Ashtakavarga
  - final chapters: remedies (*shanti*)

### Maharishi Jaimini: *Upadeśa Sūtras* (Jaimini Sutras)
- About 1,000 terse aphorisms in 4 *adhyāyas*. They use the *kaṭapayādi* letter-number code, so the sutras need commentaries (Nilakantha, Somanatha, Krishnananda Saraswati; in English, Iranganti Rangacharya and Sanjay Rath).
- They give a distinct system built on **chara (variable) karakas** (Atmakaraka and the rest), **rashi drishti** (sign aspects), **arudha padas**, **argala** (planetary intervention), and **sign-based dashas** (Chara, Narayana, Sthira, Shoola, Niryana Shoola, among others).
- Parashara's Light shows chara karakas and arudhas in Jaimini style.

### Varāhamihira (d. c. 587 CE, Ujjain)
- He was one of the "nine gems" of a legendary court. His *Pañcasiddhāntikā* summarises five astronomical schools, including the Greek-derived *Romaka* and *Pauliśa*.
- ***Bṛhat Jātaka*** (28 chapters in the usual recension) is the most respected compact textbook of natal astrology, covering planets, signs, strength, longevity, yogas and death. Commentaries include Bhattotpala's (10th century); in English there are translations by V. Subrahmanya Sastri and B. Suryanarain Rao.
- ***Bṛhat Saṃhitā*** (about 106 chapters) is the classic of mundane astrology and natural omens: planetary transits through nakshatras, eclipses, comets, rainfall, earthquakes, architecture (*vāstu*), gems, physiognomy. Translations by M. Ramakrishna Bhat and N. C. Iyer are standard.
- Also attributed to him: *Laghu Jātaka*, *Yoga Yātrā* (military electional astrology) and *Vivāha Paṭala* (marriage). He cites the Yavana authorities by name.

### Pṛthuyaśas (son of Varahamihira)
- ***Ṣaṭpañcāśikā*** ("56 verses") is a short, influential horary (*praśna*) manual; the English translation by V. Subrahmanya Sastri is the usual one.
- ***Horāsāra*** is a natal work that follows his father's methods.

### Satyācārya
- He is an earlier authority whom Varahamihira repeatedly quotes and prefers, especially on **longevity** (*āyurdāya*) and the use of navamsha and nakshatra. His *Satya Jātaka* survives only in quotations and later reconstructions (*Satya Saṃhitā*). His name is attached to nakshatra-based techniques and to the Nadi tradition.

### Kalyāṇa Varmā: *Sārāvalī* (c. 9th–10th century)
- A large compendium (about 54 chapters) that expands on Varahamihira, with especially full treatment of **planetary combinations**: results of two-, three- and four-planet conjunctions, planets in each sign and house, and lunar yogas. R. Santhanam's translation is the usual one.

### Mantreśvara: *Phaladīpikā* (c. 13th–16th century, South India)
- A clear 28-chapter manual that many teachers recommend as the first classical text. It covers dignity, house significations, yogas, Ashtakavarga, and **gochara with vedha** (ch. 26). Its chapter on bhava significations (ch. 1) is often quoted. Translations by V. Subrahmanya Sastri and G. S. Kapoor.

### Vaidyanātha Dīkṣita: *Jātaka Pārijāta* (c. 15th century)
- An 18-chapter synthesis drawing on Varahamihira, Saravali and others. It is strong on yogas (Raja, Dhana, Nabhasa), longevity, female horoscopy and **Kalachakra dasha**. Subrahmanya Sastri's 3-volume translation is the usual one.

### Kālidāsa: *Uttara Kālāmṛta* (c. 16th–17th century)
- Traditionally credited to "Kalidasa", though probably not the classical poet. The text is in 7 *khaṇḍas*. It is famous for its exhaustive lists of house and planet significations (section IV), which are widely quoted in modern textbooks, and for its treatment of the Upagrahas and the Jaimini-style special lagnas.

### Veṅkaṭeśa Daivajña: *Sarvārtha Cintāmaṇi* (date uncertain, probably c. 13th–16th century)
- A practical text organised house by house, with especially detailed rules for **dasha and antardasha results** and for wealth and longevity. J. N. Sharma's translation is the usual one.

**Natural relationships** (BPHS ch. 3):
- Sun: friends Moon, Mars, Jupiter; enemies Venus, Saturn
- Moon: friends Sun, Mercury; no enemies
- Mars: friends Sun, Moon, Jupiter; enemy Mercury
- Mercury: friends Sun, Venus; enemy Moon
- Jupiter: friends Sun, Moon, Mars; enemies Mercury, Venus
- Venus: friends Mercury, Saturn; enemies Sun, Moon
- Saturn: friends Mercury, Venus; enemies Sun, Moon, Mars

Any pair not listed is neutral.

**Temporary relationships:** planets in the 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 10th, 11th or 12th sign from a planet are its temporary friends; all others are temporary enemies.

**Compound (fivefold) relationship:**
- friend + friend = great friend
- friend + enemy = neutral
- neutral + friend = friend
- neutral + enemy = enemy
- enemy + enemy = great enemy

This compound relationship is what software prints as "Grt.Fr / Frnd / Neutr / Enemy / Grt.En".

**Varga groups** (Shadvarga, Saptavarga, Dashavarga, Shodashavarga) feed **Vimshopaka bala**, a score out of 20 for how consistently dignified a planet is across the vargas. A planet in the same sign in D1 and D9 is **vargottama**, which is considered strong.

Rules worth knowing:
- **D3 (Parashari):** the first 10° of a sign map to the same sign, 10–20° to the 5th from it, and 20–30° to the 9th.
- **D9:** count from Aries for fire signs, Capricorn for earth signs, Libra for air signs and Cancer for water signs.

### 6. Dashas (planetary periods)

#### 6.1 Vimshottari (BPHS ch. 46): the workhorse
- **Cycle and years.** The full cycle is 120 years: Ketu 7, Venus 20, Sun 6, Moon 10, Mars 7, Rahu 18, Jupiter 16, Saturn 19, Mercury 17.
- **Starting period.** The mahadasha running at birth belongs to the lord of the Moon's nakshatra.
- **Balance at birth.**
  `balance = (1 − elapsed fraction of the nakshatra) × years of its lord`, where
  `elapsed fraction = (Moon longitude − start of nakshatra) / 13°20′`
- **Sub-periods.** The antardasha (bhukti) of planet B within mahadasha A lasts `A_years × B_years / 120` years, in the same sequence starting from A itself. Pratyantardashas subdivide each antardasha the same way.
- **Worked example (reference chart).**
  - The Moon at Virgo 28°32′ is in Chitra (Mars), 5°12′ into its 13°20′ span. That is 39.0% elapsed and 61.0% remaining.
  - Mars balance = 0.61 × 7 = **4.27 years**, so the Mars dasha ends about late September 2003.
  - Rahu (18 years) then runs to about late September or early October 2021.
  - **Jupiter (16 years) runs about Oct 2021 to Oct 2037.**
  - Saturn (19 years) runs to about 2056.
  - Within Jupiter: Jupiter–Jupiter to Nov 2023, Jupiter–Saturn to about May–Jun 2026, Jupiter–Mercury about Jun 2026 to Sep 2028 (16 × 17/120 = 2.27 years), then Ketu, Venus, Sun, Moon, Mars and Rahu.
  - Dates shift by a few days depending on year-length convention and the exact Moon degree.

#### 6.2 Yogini dasha
- **Cycle.** 36 years, divided among 8 *yoginis*: Mangala (Moon) 1, Pingala (Sun) 2, Dhanya (Jupiter) 3, Bhramari (Mars) 4, Bhadrika (Mercury) 5, Ulka (Saturn) 6, Siddha (Venus) 7, Sankata (Rahu) 8.
- **Starting yogini.** (nakshatra number + 3), divided by 8; the remainder gives the yogini (a remainder of 0 means Sankata).
- **Reference chart.** Chitra is nakshatra 14: 14 + 3 = 17, and 17 mod 8 = 1, so the chart starts in Mangala. The balance is 0.61 years, ending about Jan 2000. Siddha (Venus) runs about 2020–2027, then Sankata (Rahu) about 2027–2035.
- **Use.** It is popular as a cross-check on Vimshottari, especially in North India.

#### 6.3 Chara dasha (Jaimini)
- **Basis.** Signs, not planets, run the periods.
- **Starting sign and direction.** The sequence starts from the Lagna. It runs forward or backward according to the sign group (Jaimini's *pada* rules; schools differ, e.g. K. N. Rao's vs Sanjay Rath's variations).
- **Length.** Each sign's period equals the count from that sign to its lord, minus 1, giving 1–12 years.
- **Interpretation.** Results are read from the chara karakas and arudhas relative to the dasha sign.

#### 6.4 Kalachakra dasha
- **Basis.** It is based on the **nakshatra pada** of the Moon, with *savya* (direct) and *apasavya* (reverse) cycles of signs that have fixed years (e.g. Aries 7, Taurus 16, Gemini 9 …).
- **Status.** BPHS calls it the most important dasha. It is computationally intricate, with "jumps" (*gatis*) between signs. Jagannatha Hora and Kala implement it.

#### 6.5 Others
- Ashtottari (108 years, conditional)
- Narayana and Shoola (Jaimini; Shoola is used for longevity)
- Moola, Sudarshana Chakra (yearly progression from Lagna, Moon and Sun)
- Mudda and Patyayini (for the annual *varshaphala* chart, *Tajika*)

### 7. Ashtakavarga (BPHS ch. 66–72)
- **Bhinnashtakavarga (BAV).** Each of the 7 planets gets its own table. Eight reference points (the 7 planets plus Lagna) each award a **bindu** (benefic point) to certain houses counted from themselves. The fixed totals per planet are: Sun 48, Moon 49, Mars 39, Mercury 54, Jupiter 56, Venus 52, Saturn 39.
- **Sarvashtakavarga (SAV),** also called *samudaya*: the sum across signs. It **always totals 337**, an average of about 28 per sign.
  - Signs above 28 are strong; transits through them are said to go well.
  - Signs below 25 are weak.
  - A common rule: 11th-house bindus greater than 12th-house bindus means income exceeds expenditure.
- **Reductions.** *Trikona shodhana* and *ekadhipatya shodhana* refine the tables for longevity and other calculations.
- **Kakshya (kaksha).** Each sign is split into 8 strips of 3°45′, ruled in order by **Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Sun, Venus, Mercury, Moon, Lagna**. A transiting planet is said to give good results while it crosses a strip whose ruler contributed a bindu to that planet's BAV in that sign.
- **Prastara.** The full grid of who gave which bindu.

### 8. Shadbala (BPHS ch. 27)
Six sources of strength, measured in *virupas* (60 virupas = 1 *rupa*):
1. **Sthana bala** (positional): uccha, saptavargaja, ojayugma, kendradi, drekkana
2. **Dig bala** (directional): Jupiter and Mercury in the 1st, Sun and Mars in the 10th, Saturn in the 7th, Moon and Venus in the 4th
3. **Kala bala** (temporal): day or night, paksha, tribhaga, year/month/day/hour lords, ayana, yuddha
4. **Cheshta bala** (motional): retrogression gives high cheshta
5. **Naisargika bala** (natural): fixed values, Sun the highest and Saturn the lowest
6. **Drik bala** (aspectual): aspects from benefics minus aspects from malefics

**Minimum required (rupas):** Sun 6.5, Moon 6, Mars 5, Mercury 7, Jupiter 6.5, Venus 5.5, Saturn 5. Software usually prints a **ratio = actual ÷ required**: above 1.0 is sufficient, below 1.0 is weak.

**Bhava bala** applies the same idea to houses. **Ishta and kashta phala** (from uccha and cheshta bala) estimate the good versus bad results a planet gives.

### 9. Yogas
Combinations with named results. BPHS, *Saravali*, *Phaladeepika* and Raman's *Three Hundred Important Combinations* are the standard sources.

- **Pancha Mahapurusha** (*Brihat Jataka* ch. 13; BPHS ch. 75 in some editions): Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus or Saturn in its own or exaltation sign *and* in a kendra. These give Ruchaka, Bhadra, Hamsa, Malavya and Shasha respectively.
- **Raja yogas:** a kendra lord combined with a trikona lord by conjunction, aspect or exchange. A **yogakaraka** is one planet that owns both a kendra and a trikona (e.g. Venus for Capricorn and Aquarius lagnas, Saturn for Taurus and Libra, Mars for Cancer and Leo).
- **Dhana yogas:** lords of 1, 2, 5, 9 and 11 combining.
- **Viparita Raja yogas:** lords of 6, 8 and 12 placed in dusthanas, called Harsha (6th lord), Sarala (8th lord) and Vimala (12th lord).
- **Neecha-bhanga Raja yoga:** cancellation of debilitation, for example when the lord of the debilitation sign, or of the exaltation sign, is in a kendra from the Lagna or the Moon (*Phaladeepika* ch. 7).
- **Lunar yogas:**
  - Gajakesari: Jupiter in a kendra from the Moon
  - Sunapha, Anapha, Durudhara: planets in the 2nd and/or 12th from the Moon
  - Kemadruma: nothing in the 2nd or 12th from the Moon, considered adverse
  - Chandra-Mangala: Moon with Mars
- **Solar yogas:** Vesi, Vasi, Ubhayachari, and Budha-Aditya (Sun with Mercury).
- **Nabhasa yogas** (BPHS ch. 35): 32 pattern-based configurations of the whole chart (Rajju, Musala, Nala, Gada, Yupa and others).

### 10. Karakas (significators)
- **Naisargika (natural) karakas:** see the planet table above.
- **Bhava karakas:** 1 Sun, 2 Jupiter, 3 Mars, 4 Moon (also Mercury), 5 Jupiter, 6 Saturn/Mars, 7 Venus, 8 Saturn, 9 Jupiter (also Sun), 10 Sun/Mercury/Jupiter/Saturn, 11 Jupiter, 12 Saturn.
- **Chara (Jaimini) karakas** (BPHS ch. 32; Jaimini Sutras 1.1):
  - Rank the planets by degree *within their sign*, highest to lowest. Rahu, when included, is counted backwards (30° minus his degree).
  - **7-karaka scheme** (Sun–Saturn): Atmakaraka (AK, the soul), Amatyakaraka (AmK, minister or career), Bhratrikaraka (BK, siblings), Matrikaraka (MK, mother), Putrakaraka (PK, children), Gnatikaraka (GK, rivals and disease), Darakaraka (DK, spouse).
  - **8-karaka scheme** (adds Rahu): inserts Pitrikaraka (PiK, father) after MK.
  - Parashara's Light can display either. Its karaka column depends on that setting, so check which scheme it is using.
  - The AK's sign in the navamsha is the **Karakamsha**, which is central to Jaimini's spiritual and career analysis.

### 11. Arudhas (Jaimini Sutras 1.3; BPHS ch. 29–30)
- **Calculation.** The arudha (*pada*) of a house is found by counting from the house to its lord, then counting the same number again from the lord.
- **Exception.** If the result falls in the house itself or the 7th from it, take the 10th from that result. Most modern software applies this by default; PL lets you toggle it.
- **Meaning.** The arudha shows how a matter *appears* or manifests in the world (image, perception, material form), as opposed to its "real" substance in the bhava.
  - **AL** (Arudha Lagna) = the public image.
  - **A7** (Darapada) = partners and business.
  - **A12** (*Upapada*, UL) = marriage.
  - Parashara's Light labels them 2P … 12P.

### 12. Avasthas (planetary states, BPHS ch. 45)
- **Baladi (age) avasthas** split each sign into five 6° strips:
  - Odd signs: Bala (infant) 0–6°, Kumara (adolescent) 6–12°, Yuva (youth or prime) 12–18°, Vriddha (old) 18–24°, Mrita (dead) 24–30°.
  - Even signs run in reverse order.
  - Yuva gives full results, Kumara about half, Bala a quarter, Vriddha very little and Mrita none.
- **Jagradadi:** awake (own or exalted sign), dreaming (friend's or neutral sign), asleep (enemy's or debilitation sign).
- **Deeptadi (9 states):** Deepta (exalted), Swastha (own sign), Mudita (great friend), Shanta, Dina, Vikala (combust), Khala, Kopa, and so on.
- **Lajjitadi (6) and Shayanadi (12):** more elaborate state systems.

### 13. Gochara (transits)
- **Reference point.** In Jyotish transits are counted chiefly from the **natal Moon** (Chandra lagna); the Lagna is used secondarily.
- **Favourable houses from the Moon** (*Phaladeepika* ch. 26):

  | Planet | Favourable houses from the Moon |
  |---|---|
  | Sun | 3, 6, 10, 11 |
  | Moon | 1, 3, 6, 7, 10, 11 |
  | Mars | 3, 6, 11 |
  | Mercury | 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 11 |
  | Jupiter | 2, 5, 7, 9, 11 |
  | Venus | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 9, 11, 12 |
  | Saturn, Rahu, Ketu | 3, 6, 11 |

- **Vedha:** a favourable transit is obstructed when another planet occupies its paired *vedha* point.
- **Sade Sati:** Saturn's roughly 7½-year passage through the 12th, 1st and 2nd from the natal Moon.
- **Refinements:** Ashtakavarga bindus and kakshas, and the double transit.
- **Reference chart.** The Moon is in Virgo, so Sade Sati begins when Saturn enters sidereal Leo, roughly in the late 2030s. Use an ephemeris for exact dates.

### 14. Muhurta (electional astrology)
- **Method.** Choose the moment that starts an undertaking.
- **Pañcāṅga** ("five limbs"): *tithi* (lunar day), *vāra* (weekday), *nakṣatra*, *yoga* (Sun + Moon longitude ÷ 13°20′) and *karaṇa* (half-tithi).
- **Further checks:** *tārābala* (nakshatra compatibility with the birth star), *candrabala* (Moon's house from the natal Moon), and avoidance of Rahu kala, Gulika kala, Vishti karana and eclipses. Strong lagnas are preferred, and Abhijit muhurta around local noon is favoured.
- **Classic texts:** *Muhurta Chintamani*, *Kalaprakashika*, Varahamihira's *Yoga Yatra* and *Vivaha Patala*; in English, B. V. Raman's *Muhurtha* and Ernst Wilhelm's *Classical Muhurta*.

### 15. Prashna (horary)
- **Method.** A chart is cast for the moment a question is asked, or received.
- **Schools:**
  - Pṛthuyaśas' *Ṣaṭpañcāśikā*
  - *Daivajña Vallabha* (attributed to Varahamihira)
  - *Praśna Mārga*, Kerala's comprehensive manual with omens (*nimitta*), *Ashtamangala* prashna and *arudha* by number
  - **KP horary**: the querent gives a number from 1 to 249 that sets the ascendant sub-lord
  - *Tājika praśna* (Perso-Arabic aspects)
